SEOUL — AUGUST 2026

From Love Dive
to Blackhole.

IVE's second world tour, Show What I Am, kicked off at Seoul's KSPO Dome in October 2025 and has been running alongside a full-length comeback ever since. February brought second studio album REVIVE+ and its double title tracks, "BLACKHOLE" and "BANG BANG" — a comeback one outlet described as IVE "shedding her princess image and expanding her worldview." "BLACKHOLE" went on to take first place on Show Champion in March. The tour itself kept climbing: a second Tokyo Dome run in June drew roughly 95,000 fans and front-page coverage in all five of Japan's major sports newspapers, and Hong Kong and Taipei dates are next on the calendar. This page is a working build of how IVE Wire tracks all of it for English-speaking DIVE — translated, sourced, and converted to whatever timezone you're reading from.

A note on this era: REVIVE+ is IVE's second full-length album and carries two title tracks rather than one, plus a solo track for each of the group's six members (Gaeul, Yujin, Rei, Wonyoung, Liz, Leeseo). It arrived roughly three years after the group's 2021 debut single "Eleven," while the Show What I Am tour that carries it continues into the fall.

TOKYO — JUN 24

Second Tokyo Dome run draws 95,000 fans and front-page coverage

4.0 / 5EDITOR'S STAMP
"A double-title-track album that spends its runtime shedding the princess image and expanding the worldview — 'BLACKHOLE' is the sound of a group betting it can carry two leads at once, and mostly winning."

REVIVE+ (2026): IVE's second full-length album, and its most structurally ambitious release yet. Twelve tracks split across two title songs — "BANG BANG," pre-released ahead of the album, and the cinematic "BLACKHOLE" — bookend a tracklist that also carves out a solo spotlight for all six members, from Wonyoung's "8" to Yujin's "FORCE." "BLACKHOLE" charted internationally on release and later took first place on Show Champion.

The two-lead-single structure is a genuine risk for a group whose earlier eras built around one clear anthem at a time, but it pays off by giving the album a wider emotional range than a typical mini could — moody and expansive on the title tracks, more personal on the solo cuts.
